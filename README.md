# Livora

**A personal library and reading companion designed to help readers organize, remember, and reflect on what they read.**

Livora started as a product I built for myself.

I wanted more than a place to track finished books. I wanted a system where my library, notes, reviews, collections, and reading history could live together — and become more valuable over time.

The core idea is simple:

> **A personal library should remember more than the books you own. It should remember what they gave you.**

## The product

Livora is built around three parts of the reading experience:

**Organize**  
Manage books through reading status, shelves, and custom collections.

**Read**  
Keep notes, progress, and reading activity connected to each book.

**Remember**  
Preserve quotations, reflections, reviews, and ideas long after a book is finished.

## Core features

The current V1 includes:

- personal library management;
- reading status and book lifecycle;
- shelves and custom collections;
- book pages with reading history;
- notes and commonplace entries;
- ratings and written reviews;
- reading dashboards and reflection tools.

The focus is deliberately narrow: build a reading experience worth using before adding more complex automation or AI.

## Product principles

Livora follows a few simple principles:

- **The reader comes before the metric.** Reading is more than a yearly book count.
- **Organization should be personal.** A library should reflect the way its owner thinks.
- **Reading does not end on the last page.** Books continue through notes, rereads, research, and connections.
- **AI should support reading, not replace it.** Future AI features should help readers explore their own library and ideas without reducing books to summaries.
- **The product should become more valuable with time.** Years of reading history should form a meaningful record of a reader's intellectual life.

## Building from actual use

Livora was first built for a single user: me.

Using it as part of my own reading routine has shaped the product more than hypothetical feature lists could. Some assumptions survived. Others changed once real books, notes, and reading history began accumulating.

The development loop has been simple:

**build → use → observe → revise**

The next step is to introduce a small group of users and see how those workflows hold up outside my own library.

## What comes next

Future directions include PWA and AI-assisted library exploration, connections between books and ideas, richer reading goals, better reflection, and selective sharing of notes and reviews.

These are directions rather than a fixed feature roadmap. Livora continues to evolve from actual use.
